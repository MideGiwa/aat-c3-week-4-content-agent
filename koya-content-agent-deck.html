Content quality isn't the problem.
Scale is.

The content team already has a pipeline that works: brainstorm an idea, research it, write it, adapt it for every channel, review it, publish it. It just can't run any faster without more people.

The existing pipeline, today

Idea → research → SEO article → LinkedIn / X / newsletter adaptation → internal review → publish — done by hand, start to finish, for every single piece.

Fully manual Doesn't scale with headcount held flat Tone & accuracy slip under volume pressure

The real constraint: growing output today means hiring more people — or accepting worse quality, tone drift, and factual risk as the team pushes harder.

What can't be compromised

  • Every piece of content must stay traceable to real sources — not just "we looked at a few links."
  • Nothing publishes without a human decision — approval is a gate, not a formality.
✓

Goal: an agent that takes over the repetitive middle of the pipeline — research, drafting, self-checking, channel adaptation — while a human still makes the final call before anything goes out.

1 / 3 — Business Problem
The Solution

An agent for the repetitive middle.
A human for the final call.

One staged pipeline, triggered by a simple request, that researches and drafts largely on its own, checks its own work, and only then hands off for a human decision.

How a request moves through the system

  1. Submit a request
    An idea or a source URL, plus audience, tone, and which channels it's for.
  2. Research & curate sources
    Scrapes or searches for reference material, ranks it, and keeps every claim traceable to the exact excerpt that supports it.
  3. Draft, then self-check
    Drafts the piece, scores it against a fixed rubric, and automatically revises any section that falls short — capped, so it can't loop forever.
  4. Adapt for every channel
    LinkedIn, X, and newsletter versions are generated together from the same approved draft.
  5. Human review
    A reviewer approves, rejects, or requests changes — a change request triggers its own tracked, automatic revision round.
  6. Publishing queue
    Approved content lands in a queue, tagged by channel, ready to go out immediately or on a schedule.

What stays automatic

Research & source ranking Drafting Self-evaluation & revision Channel adaptation

What stays human

Approve / reject / request changes What gets published, and when
🔎

Every stage writes to an activity log — inputs, sources used, evaluation scores — so any piece of content, and any failure, is traceable end to end.
